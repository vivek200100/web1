Damage begins

01 / The challenge

Damage does not
act alone.

A flexural crack opens a route for aggressive ions. Localized corrosion turns a small imperfection into a stress concentrator. Cyclic loading then activates it. The consequence is a feedback loop—not a sequence of separate damage events.

02 / Coupled mechanics

From an anodic flaw
to a structural forecast.

The evolving pit-to-crack flaw is treated as a living state variable. Its geometry changes the local driving forces; those forces govern crack advance, reinforcement loss, bond deterioration, stiffness decay, and ultimately remaining life.

03 / Damage geometry

From pulses
to a crack.

Probabilistic acoustic-emission classification separates highly probable tensile events from uncertainty. Their localized sequence is then converted into crack path, crack length, and three-dimensional damage geometry.

04 / The sensing problem

The environment changes the message.

Acoustic emission carries evidence of active fracture. In submerged concrete, water-filled pores and fluid boundaries preferentially suppress the high-frequency content that identifies tensile cracking. A true fracture can arrive at the sensor looking like noise.

05 / X-CON

Recover before
you classify.

X-CON is a deterministic, physics-informed recovery and consensus framework. It first corrects distance- and frequency-dependent attenuation, then evaluates the reconstructed signal across independent temporal and spectral signatures.

06 / Towards prognosis

From hidden damage
to defensible decisions.

The aim is a continuous chain of evidence: understand the coupled mechanism, recover the signal, estimate the evolving crack state, and translate local damage into structural serviceability and remaining-life prognosis.
